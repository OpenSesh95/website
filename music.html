Music

The lifetime layer

Before any of the current rotation, there was the archive every millennial ear is built from. Classic rock and prog at the root — Pink Floyd above everything; if a stranger needs one song to understand the taste, it's still "Shine On You Crazy Diamond." AC/DC and Coldplay in the heavy rotation. Hip-hop through Eminem. Linkin Park for the years when everything was hybrid. The 2010s layer — pop, EDM, lofi in the background of a decade of work — sits in the archive too, unedited.

The archive is literal: my SoundCloud holds eight playlists and more than three and a half thousand tracks — a favorites set, and behind it a full backup, because apparently I collect music the way I collect everything else: complete runs, with a manifest.

Some of it is a maker's bench, not a listening list — tracks flagged for sampling over the years: Daft Punk twice over, Stardust's "Music Sounds Better With You," Tinlicker's "Children," deadmau5's "Strobe." A producer's ear was growing up alongside the listener's.

The current layer

Electronic first, now. Ben Böhmer for the melodic end — music that takes its time. Fred Again.., built from voice notes and real life, the most human electronic music I know. Boris Brejcha and Liquid Stranger for the harder edge, when the melodic end gets too polite. Bollywood underneath it all, lifelong — and lately bolly tech, After All the name on that shelf. And the devotional shelf: M.S. Subbulakshmi, Bhaja Govindam — not background music; the listening everything else sits on.

The anchor I follow

@cdr_only — "Midnight Transmission" — is an account I follow, not me: trance, progressive, and deep cuts from the nineties forward, remembered properly. Original-era versions over remixes, the physical artifact over the stream. My electronic collecting runs against that standard first; my ear decides after.
