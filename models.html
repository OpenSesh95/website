Models

Findings from running AI systems — written for the public, one piece at a time. Each piece names the public work it rests on.

The discipline is the system

Two small systems I built and published taught me more about running AI than any model release this year. Neither is about the model. Both are about what surrounds it.

The first wraps every model call in a control loop: the output is checked by plain deterministic rules before a person ever sees it, it stops at an approval gate that stays closed unless someone opens it, and every run — approved, rejected, or failed — is written to a ledger with its cost. The second decides what goes into the model's context in the first place: given a question and a pile of files, it packs only what fits a stated budget, shows its accounting, and reports what it left out and why, instead of silently cutting.

Four findings, stated generally, because they generalize:

Verify before review. A human's attention is the scarcest resource in the system. Deterministic checks — size, shape, required terms, valid structure — cost nothing and should reject everything they can before a person spends judgment on the rest.

A gate that defaults open is not a gate. In both systems, nothing passes by default. An unattended run that nobody approved is recorded as a rejection, not waved through. That single choice changes what the records mean: an approval in the ledger is evidence, not an assumption.

Keep the failures in the ledger. Most tooling records successes and lets failures evaporate. Metering every outcome — including the rejections and the provider errors, with token counts taken from the provider's own usage numbers, never estimated — turns a demo into an operation. You cannot improve a system whose failures you didn't keep.

Context is a budget, not a pile. Packing deterministically — same inputs, same pack, every run — with the exclusions reported rather than hidden, makes the model's input something you can reason about and reproduce. The moment packing becomes vibes, debugging becomes archaeology.

The models will keep changing. The loop around them is the part that compounds.

The two systems are public: gated-dispatch — dispatch, verify, gate, ledger. context-router — deterministic context packing under a budget.
